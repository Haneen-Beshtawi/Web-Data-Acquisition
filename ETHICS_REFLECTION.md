# Ethics reflection

For this project, I collected the Predict Students' Dropout and Academic Success dataset from the UCI Machine Learning Repository. I used its documented API workflow to retrieve the metadata and then download the CSV identified by the response. Both requests succeeded without an account or API key. The dataset is licensed under CC BY 4.0, so I credited its creators and described my changes. I preserved the original files and recorded the collection times and request details. Reusing the saved snapshot avoids unnecessary traffic to the repository.

The records concern real students, which creates privacy concerns even though direct personal identifiers are not provided. The original dataset includes demographic, family, financial and academic information. Combining unusual combinations of these attributes with outside information could increase the risk of identifying someone. My cleaned dataset keeps only the academic fields needed for the question, such as semester grades, units approved and course outcome. I did not link records to social media, institutional directories or other personal information. The source-row reference is only a position in the saved file, not a student identifier.

A major risk is treating the outcome labels as judgments about a person's ability or motivation. Students may leave higher education because of financial pressure, health, family responsibilities or other circumstances that the selected academic fields do not explain. An institution could misuse these data to deny admission or support to students predicted to drop out. A more responsible purpose would be evaluating supportive services, with appropriate oversight and current local evidence.

I reported descriptive group comparisons rather than claims of causation. The dataset reflects one institutional setting, and its creators already performed preprocessing. It may not represent students at other universities or current cohorts. Semester results are not available at admission, so using them for admission-time predictions would leak future information. I also checked missingness, numeric ranges and outcome labels rather than assuming that a successful download guarantees valid analysis. Public availability permits access under the license, but does not remove my responsibility to protect learners and explain these limitations.
